Part 1 — Basic Company Research

What the business does, who runs it, and whether its products should remain relevant.

Company background and history

Lockheed Martin is a U.S. aerospace, defense, and security company that designs and manufactures military aircraft, missiles, air-defense systems, helicopters, satellites, and space technology. Its current form was created in 1995 when Lockheed Corporation and Martin Marietta merged. The company is headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland, and sells to the U.S. government as well as allied countries.

Defense programs usually run for many years. That can give Lockheed Martin a more predictable stream of work than businesses that rely on individual consumers. However, a large part of its business depends on government budgets, contract terms, and its ability to deliver equipment on time and within cost.

Is the company growing?

Recent results suggest growth. In the third quarter of 2026, sales reached $20.7 billion, compared with $18.6 billion in Q3 2025, while net earnings increased to about $1.8 billion from $1.6 billion. The company also reported a record $292 billion backlog and raised its 2026 sales and earnings outlook. These are positive signs, although backlog is not the same as cash already earned; the company still has to produce and deliver the work.

Who is the CEO?

Jim Taiclet is chairman, president, and CEO. He became CEO in June 2020 and chairman in March 2021.

Taiclet previously led American Tower Corporation, where he helped expand it into a global communications infrastructure business. Earlier, he held leadership roles at AlliedSignal Aerospace Services and Pratt & Whitney. He also served as a U.S. Air Force officer and pilot and studied at the U.S. Air Force Academy and Princeton University.

My assessment: His experience in both aerospace and running a large company is a positive. American Tower's growth under his leadership is evidence of past business success, but it does not guarantee that every Lockheed program will meet its targets.

Top 3 products and programs

1. F-35 Lightning II: A stealth fighter used by the U.S. and allied militaries. It is likely to remain important because air forces need modern aircraft, training, upgrades, spare parts, and maintenance over decades. A 10-year framework for at least 850 jets was announced in October 2026, but formal terms and funding still depend on government action.

2. PAC-3 MSE: A missile interceptor used in the Patriot air-defense system to stop incoming ballistic missiles and other threats. Countries need air defense to protect people, bases, and infrastructure, so demand could remain high while security concerns continue.

3. THAAD: A missile-defense system designed to intercept ballistic missiles at high altitude. It is strategically important as countries invest in layered missile defense. These are defense products rather than ordinary consumer goods, so purchases depend on government needs and budgets.

Can Lockheed Martin survive and grow?

Yes, I feel reasonably confident that the company will remain important over the long term. It supplies complex defense systems that governments cannot replace quickly, has long-running programs, and reported a large backlog. The F-35, PAC-3, and THAAD programs are likely to remain relevant as countries modernize their militaries.

However, I would not call it risk-free. It depends heavily on government customers, faces cost overruns and production challenges, and can be affected by political decisions. My confidence is in the durability of the business, not a guarantee that its share price will always rise.

Part 3 — Final Recommendation

Top 3 reasons to buy LMT

  1. Strong demand and backlog. The $292 billion backlog provides visibility into future work, and the company raised its 2026 outlook.
  2. Important long-term products. Fighter jets and missile-defense systems are likely to remain priorities for the U.S. and allied governments.
  3. Positive chart momentum and dividends. Recent prices rose with stronger volume, and Lockheed Martin has a history of paying regular dividends. Dividends can change and are not guaranteed.

Top 3 reasons NOT to buy LMT

  1. Government dependence. Budgets, elections, contract approvals, and funding delays can affect revenue and cash flow.
  2. Execution and cost risk. Large defense programs can experience production delays, supply shortages, cost overruns, and contract disputes. Lockheed specifically noted unresolved funding authorization for newer F-35 lots.
  3. Price and valuation risk. The stock has climbed sharply in October. Even a strong company can be a disappointing investment if bought at too high a price or if expectations are already reflected in the share price.

My final recommendation: Cautious buy for a 1+ year investor

Based on my research, I would consider buying Lockheed Martin for a long-term portfolio, but I would not put all my money in at once after the recent price jump. The company reported 11% year-over-year quarterly sales growth, a record $292 billion backlog, and increased its 2026 outlook. Its F-35 and missile-defense programs could support demand for years, and the recent stock chart is bullish because prices have risen alongside higher trading volume. On the other hand, government funding and contract execution are important risks, and the stock could pull back after its rapid run-up. My decision is a cautious buy for someone who can hold through volatility, while checking valuation and spreading purchases over time.
